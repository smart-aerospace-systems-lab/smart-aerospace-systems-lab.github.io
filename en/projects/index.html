  • 2020.06 – 2027.05

    Development of Structural Technologies for Biomimetic Unmanned Vehicles

    Funding
    Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) · Unmanned Vehicles Core Technology R&D Program
    Partners
    KAIST, Korea National University of Transportation, Chungnam National University, Korea Aerospace University, and others
    Our role
    Structural technologies for flapping-wing vehicles
    Lead
    Led by KAIST (PI: Jae-Hung Han)
    Team
    Flapping-Wing Aerial Vehicle
    Project no.
    2710101848

    For next-generation biomimetic unmanned vehicles modeled on the body structure and motion of birds, the project develops morphing-wing and flapping-wing structures and actuation mechanisms and validates them with prototypes in real operating environments.

    Objectives

    • Novel structural concepts and application technologies for next-generation biomimetic unmanned vehicles
    • Morphing and flapping actuation mechanisms based on biological motion for efficient locomotion and attitude control
    • An integrated simulation framework covering nonlinear structures, unsteady aerodynamics and multibody dynamics, with validation
    • Energy-minimizing, robust control algorithms for long-endurance operation and flight in extreme environments
    • Prototypes of a compound morphing-wing vehicle and a flapping-wing vehicle, validated in real operating environments (TRL 6)
    • Biomimetic Unmanned Vehicle
    • Biomimetic Structure
    • Morphing Wing Air Vehicle
    • Flapping Wing Air Vehicle
    • Integrated Simulation Framework

  • 2024.04 – 2026.12

    Development of Design and Construction Technologies for Portable Modular Vertiports and a Surveillance System

    Funding
    Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (MOLIT) · Korea Agency for Infrastructure Technology Advancement (KAIA) · K-UAM Safe Operation System Core Technology Development Program
    Partners
    Hyundai Elevator, Korea National University of Transportation, Cheongju University, Ulsan Information Technology Industry Promotion Agency, Korea Testing Certification Institute (KTC), Ninano Company, Lotte E&C
    Our role
    UAM take-off/landing surveillance system and auxiliary positioning technology
    Lead
    Led by Hyundai Elevator Co., Ltd.
    Team
    Surveillance System
    Project no.
    2610000202

    Develops vertiport design and construction technologies and a precision UAM take-off/landing surveillance system for the safe operation of Korean urban air mobility. The project builds a prototype portable, modular vertiport with vertical aircraft storage and develops a surveillance system that detects and recognizes approaching UAM aircraft and other aircraft nearby.

    Objectives

    • Standardized vertiport design criteria, operating procedures and site analysis
    • Construction of a prototype portable modular vertiport with vertical aircraft storage
    • Detailed specifications and technical standards for MW-class high-power charging systems
    • An AI vision-based surveillance system that detects, recognizes and analyzes nearby aircraft and approaching UAM
    • EO/IR/LiDAR sensor fusion and an integrated operations server
    • Vertiport
    • Elevator
    • Modular
    • Portable
    • Vertical Apron

  • 2024.04 – 2027.12

    Development and Demonstration of an Onboard Intelligent Avionics Software Platform for Recognizing and Responding to Abnormal Situations in Urban Air Mobility

    Funding
    Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) · Institute of Information & Communications Technology Planning & Evaluation (IITP) · SW Computing Industry Core Technology Development Program
    Partners
    V-SPACE, Korea National University of Transportation, Korea Electronics Technology Institute (KETI), Rehigh, Davinci Labs
    Our role
    Electric propulsion ground testing and HILS-based verification
    Lead
    Led by V-SPACE Co., Ltd.
    Team
    UAM Electric Propulsion
    Project no.
    2710008225

    Develops intelligent avionics software for UAM aircraft and validates it through flight tests. The project covers technologies that recognize and respond onboard to hazardous, emergency and failure situations during flight.

    Objectives

    • Collection of airframe, powertrain and environmental data through flight tests
    • A FACE-based avionics software framework
    • Hazard, emergency and failure detection based on real-time image/sensor fusion and multi-dimensional sensors
    • Onboard NPU-based spatial awareness and vision-based landing site recognition
    • Failure response tests for motors, ESCs, batteries and GPS
    • Application to various UAM aircraft and demonstration at the K-UAM Grand Challenge
    • Urban Air Mobility
    • Avionics
    • Software
    • Autonomous Flight
    • Remote Control

  • 2018 – 2020

    Basic Research on a Drone System for Firefighting and Rescue

    Funding
    Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) · National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF) · Basic Research Program for Individual Researchers (NRF-2018R1C1B5045891)
    Partners
    Korea National University of Transportation
    Our role
    Dynamics modeling and flight control of tethered multicopters
    Lead
    PI: Dong-Kyu Lee
    Project no.
    1711071520 · 1711091751 · 1711110383

    Studied multicopters that support firefighting and rescue in high-rise building fires beyond the reach of ladder trucks. Tethered flight, with power supplied from the ground through a cable, overcomes endurance limits, and the project analyzed how the heavy power cable affects flight.

    Objectives

    • Conceptual design of a hovering multicopter system to support early fire suppression and rescue
    • Flexible multibody dynamics analysis of a tethered multicopter, including the weight and viscoelastic bending stiffness of the power cable
    • Adaptive control (ℒ1 adaptive control) that maintains attitude and position even when the mass changes as a person boards
    • Validation of the analysis and design through scaled-model flight tests and simulation

    Key outputs

    • Modeling and Simulation of Heavy-Lift Tethered Multicopter Considering Mechanical Properties of Electric Power CableHyeok-Min Kwon, Dong-Kyu Lee · Aerospace 8(8), 208 (2021)
